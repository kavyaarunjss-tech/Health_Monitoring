Project description

Most air quality apps only show the air right now. This project forecasts the official CPCB Air Quality Index (AQI) 1 to 24 hours ahead for 8 KSPCB monitoring stations in Karnataka (Bengaluru: Silk Board, Hebbal, Jayanagar; Chikkaballapur, Chikkamagaluru, Mysuru, Ramanagara, Madikeri) and turns the forecasts into personal health alerts.

Our hybrid model CSH-AQI uses the structure of the CPCB formula: at forecast time most of each pollutant's 24-hour (or 8-hour) averaging window is already measured, so the model forecasts only the unknown hours and rebuilds the AQI with the official CPCB breakpoints, combined with gradient-boosting models in an ensemble.

Key features

Careful data cleaning

Real 15-minute sensor data (Feb 2025 – Aug 2026). Error codes, stuck sensors and spikes removed; long gaps are never filled with guesses.

Official CPCB AQI

24 h / 8 h running averages, sub-indices and the "highest sub-index" rule implemented exactly as CPCB publishes.

Hybrid forecasting model

Forecasts only the unknown hours of each averaging window, then a 6-member ensemble gives the final AQI at +1, +6, +12 and +24 h.

Personal health alerts

Alert levels by health group (lung or heart disease, children, older adults, pregnancy, outdoor workers, athletes).

Advice with sources

Every health message comes from CPCB, the Health Ministry (NCDC) or WHO, with the source shown.

Website and phone app

Dashboard, forecast chart, weekly summary, phone notifications and e-mail alerts; a replay mode shows past pollution events hour by hour.

Real-world applications

  • People with asthma, COPD or heart disease get a warning hours before Poor air, with what to do.
  • Parents, schools and older adults can plan outdoor activity for the cleaner hours of the day.
  • Outdoor workers and athletes can schedule work, breaks and exercise around forecast peaks.
  • Hospitals and city agencies can prepare for pollution episodes before they peak.

Societal impact

  • Moves air quality information from reaction (what the air is now) to prevention (what is coming).
  • Protects the most vulnerable groups with earlier, personalised and evidence-based advice.
  • Uses the official CPCB index people already see in the news, so forecasts are easy to trust and compare.
  • Supports sustainable, healthier cities (SDG 3: good health; SDG 11: sustainable cities).
